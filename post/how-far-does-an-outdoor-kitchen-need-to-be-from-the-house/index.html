How Far Does An Outdoor Kitchen Need To Be From The House?

Updated: Oct 21

An outdoor kitchen should be at least 10 feet from the house for safety, with an ideal distance of 15 to 20 feet for convenience and easy access to the indoor kitchen. 

When creating your ideal outdoor kitchen, one of the most important considerations is its distance from your house. This decision impacts safety, convenience, aesthetics, and the overall functionality of your outdoor space. Whether you’re grilling, hosting gatherings, or relaxing outdoors, the placement of your kitchen plays a key role in your cooking experience and backyard enjoyment.

At Ironhills Landscaping, we specialize in designing and installing outdoor kitchens tailored to your needs. Our expert team helps ensure that every aspect—from safety to convenience and visual appeal—is considered to create the perfect outdoor living space for you.

Key Takeaways

  • Outdoor kitchens should be at least 10 feet away from combustible materials for fire safety.

  • Position the kitchen within 15 to 20 feet of the indoor kitchen for convenience.

  • Aesthetic considerations such as views, privacy, and sunlight should influence the placement.

  • Ensure easy access to utilities like gas, water, and electricity.

  • Consider the wind direction to avoid smoke near dining areas.

  • A shaded location can improve comfort during hot weather.

How to Safely and Effectively Design Your Outdoor Kitchen: Key Considerations and Tips

Safety Considerations

When determining the location of your outdoor kitchen, safety should be your top priority. A critical rule is ensuring that your outdoor kitchen is placed at least 10 feet away from any combustible materials, including your house, deck, or other structures. This helps minimize the risk of fire hazards, especially when using grilling appliances like barbecues, brick ovens, or smokers. By maintaining this distance, you’re preventing potential heat, flames, or sparks from reaching your home or other structures.

Key Safety Guidelines:

  • Minimum 10-foot distance: Keep your outdoor kitchen at least 10 feet from your home, deck, or any flammable structures.

  • Consider large appliances: Brick ovens and smokers should also comply with the 10-foot safety rule to prevent heat transfer risks.

  • Install fire safety equipment: Equip your outdoor kitchen with a fire extinguisher and keep it easily accessible.

Convenience and Accessibility

While safety is crucial, the location of your outdoor kitchen should also prioritize convenience. Ideally, place it within 15 to 20 feet of your indoor kitchen to streamline the cooking process. This makes it easy to transport ingredients, food, and supplies between both spaces, improving your overall cooking and hosting experience. If the kitchen is too far, it may reduce its usage, as frequent trips could become frustrating.

Convenience Tips:

  • Optimal proximity: Keep your outdoor kitchen within 15 to 20 feet of your indoor kitchen for easy access.

  • Streamline cooking: Ensure direct paths between your indoor and outdoor kitchens to reduce unnecessary steps.

  • Minimize hassle: Placing your kitchen too far can discourage use—choose a location that fits your lifestyle.

Aesthetic Considerations

Beyond functionality, the placement of your outdoor kitchen should enhance the overall look and feel of your backyard. Carefully selecting the right spot can elevate your outdoor living area by complementing existing landscaping and creating a visually appealing focal point. Consider how the kitchen integrates with surrounding elements like your garden, patio, or seating areas to create a harmonious design.

Aesthetic Tips:

  • Consider views and sunlight: Position your kitchen for optimal views and lighting, ensuring it adds to the beauty of your outdoor space.

  • Maintain privacy: Place the kitchen in a way that offers privacy while still maintaining accessibility and open sightlines for guests.

  • Integrate with landscaping: Ensure your kitchen design complements the surrounding landscaping, whether through color, material choice, or theme.

Additional Factors to Consider

Several practical factors should also be taken into account when positioning your outdoor kitchen. These include access to utilities, wind direction, and providing shade for comfort. Proper planning in these areas will not only enhance the usability of your outdoor kitchen but will also make it a more comfortable and enjoyable space for cooking and entertaining.

Key Additional Factors:

  • Utilities Access: Position your kitchen near existing gas, water, and electricity lines to minimize installation costs and complexity.

  • Wind Direction: Keep in mind the direction of prevailing winds, positioning your kitchen so smoke is directed away from dining or seating areas.

  • Shade and Comfort: Choose a shaded location to keep the kitchen cool during hot weather and provide relief from the sun for you and your guests.

Designing the Perfect Outdoor Kitchen

Designing your perfect outdoor kitchen is about more than just a grill. It’s about creating a functional, inviting space with the right materials and appliances for cooking and entertaining.

Key Design Tips:

  • Durable Materials: Choose weather-resistant materials for countertops, cabinetry, and flooring.

  • Counter Space: Ensure there’s ample space for food prep and cooking.

  • Storage: Add sufficient storage for utensils, cookware, and supplies.

  • Appliances: Include outdoor-rated appliances like refrigerators, sinks, or a pizza oven.

  • Lighting: Install good lighting for evening cooking and meals.

Building an Outdoor Living Space

An outdoor living space extends your home, offering a place to relax and entertain. It should complement your style and provide a comfortable area to enjoy nature.

Key Elements to Include:

  • Functional Layout: Consider how space will be used for both relaxation and entertainment.

  • Natural Materials: Incorporate stone, wood, or plants to blend with your surroundings.

  • Versatile Features: Add fireplaces, pergolas, or seating areas for variety.

  • Reflect Your Lifestyle: Tailor the space for intimate moments or large gatherings.

Hardscaping for Outdoor Kitchens

Hardscaping enhances both the function and beauty of your outdoor kitchen. It’s essential to use durable materials that can withstand the elements, ensuring your kitchen lasts for years.

Hardscaping Tips:

  • Choose the Right Materials: Use stone, brick, or concrete for durability and aesthetics.

  • Low-Maintenance Options: Select materials that are easy to maintain and clean.

  • Weather Resistance: Ensure materials can endure heat, rain, and snow.

  • Expert Help: Work with a professional landscaper to select the best materials for your needs.

Frequently Asked Questions About Outdoor Kitchens

Does an outdoor kitchen need to be covered?While not required, covering your outdoor kitchen can protect it from the elements, extend the lifespan of appliances, and provide comfort for year-round use.

How long do outdoor kitchens last?With proper care and maintenance, outdoor kitchens can last 10-20 years or more, depending on materials and weather conditions.

Do I need planning for an outdoor kitchen?Yes, planning is essential to ensure proper placement, access to utilities, and integration with your outdoor space. Professional design helps avoid costly mistakes.

Are outdoor kitchens a good idea?Outdoor kitchens enhance your outdoor living experience, providing a space for cooking, entertaining, and enjoying nature. They can also increase home value.

What are the disadvantages of an outdoor kitchen?Disadvantages include the initial cost, potential for weather-related wear, and maintenance. It may also require more space and careful planning for utilities.
